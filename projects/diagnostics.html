Case 1 — NVR video artifacts after physical relocation

Horizontal flickering on a VGA-over-Cat6 video output after an NVR was physically moved. Delivered as a forensic log analysis report (see NVR_Forensics_Report.md, 2026-10-20): evidence acquisition → timeline reconstruction → component-boundary analysis → fault-domain isolation → ranked hypotheses with discriminating tests.

  • Evidence discipline: 20 log files acquired from the device's system-report bundle, each hashed (SHA-256) before analysis; timezone normalisation documented (device UTC+10 vs MAC table UTC); no configuration touched — conclusions from logs only.
  • Timeline from machine evidence: PoE port churn in the MAC table (camera MACs migrating between ports) independently corroborated the physical re-patching window; foreign non-camera MACs on a PoE port dated the earlier hands-on troubleshooting; a post-fault firmware upgrade was excluded as cause by timestamp ordering alone.
  • Fault-domain isolation: camera-side hypotheses dismissed via clean decode/display counters; fault follows the NVR output chain, not a channel.
  • Ranked hypotheses: leading — analog link margin: the VGA-over-Cat6 balun chain fails at 1080p60's 148.5 MHz pixel clock and stabilises at 720p60's 74.25 MHz (classic marginal-analog signature); alternative weakened-DAC hypothesis kept alive and made testable rather than hand-waved.
  • Outcome: 720p60 adopted as a documented operational mitigation; permanent fix specified (retire the analog leg for a digital HDMI extender — the HDMI chain has been fault-free on comparable wiring); intermediate timings proposed as further discriminating experiments; one test explicitly named as the decisive next probe.
