The problem, in its time

In October 2025, no mainstream model pipeline could produce a presentable document. In my judgement at the time, frontier cloud models barely formatted a table or chose a font: their "documents" were notepad output with slightly cleaner spacing. My local model was no better. Workplace deliverables I actually needed, like the BSBOPS501 training workbooks and appraisal reports, were out of reach.

LM-PDF Editor closed that gap: a prompts-and-utils pipeline in which a local model generated the content and the tool imposed professional structure, producing visually publication-quality PDFs and Word-compatible files a workplace could actually deliver. For its window, it was a leading-class proprietary application: nothing on the market did document authoring from a model's prose well.

Honest trade-offs it carried

  • Output was lossy by design: the formatted files looked good but were hard to edit afterwards, because formatting was baked in rather than semantic. In 2025 that trade bought visual quality no other pipeline could produce; by 2026 it stopped being worth paying.
  • An ethical disclaimer was published in the README from day one: "The output is AI-generated and may not be accurate or verifiable. Users are responsible for the ethical and legal use of generated content. Do not use this tool to create fake documents, impersonate individuals, or deceive others."
  • It ran entirely on a local model — workplace documents never left the machine.

Why it retired

Around four to six months after building it, the cloud model APIs gained native document authoring: proper editable formatting, tables, styles; an ordinary coding agent (no specialised tools) could now produce a better document, faster, with validation checks and live references. The capability the tool existed to compensate for stopped being special. I retired it rather than maintain a solution to a problem that no longer exists.

Durable outputs moved into the Strata agent stack. The disclaimer-to-governance ethics trajectory this artifact anchors is discussed in section 4 of the report.
